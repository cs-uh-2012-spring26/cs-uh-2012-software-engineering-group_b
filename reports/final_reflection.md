# Final Project Reflection

## Tianze Li

Throughout this project, I learned that the secret to building a truly professional system is not just writing code that works but writing code that is easy to change. Transitioning from our Sprint 3A to a structured Layered Architecture taught me the actual value of the single responsibility principle by seeing how separating the API, Service, and Database layers makes the entire codebase more readable and testable. The most fun part was definitely developing the new endpoints and testing them in the Swagger UI. Seeing the code actually run and work perfectly gave me a huge sense of accomplishment.

On the other hand, the most difficult part was the initial refactoring phase specifically when unwinding the tightly coupled logic from Sprint 3A into the specialized inheritance hierarchy the TA suggested. It required a lot of precision to ensure that moving logic into Member and Trainer subclasses did not break our existing flows. Because of this challenge I feel I am much better at architectural design and SOLID principles now. I have moved past the scripting mindset and now approach development with a design first perspective to ensure that every class and function has a clear and singular purpose.

## Vladimir

What definitely stood out to me was designing a back-end architecture without even thinking about the front-end. In the end, however, working with 'bare-bones' functionality proved to be an important exercise in functionality and modularity. 
The most fun part was breaking the large projects into sprints; by proxy of each of these mini-assignments being isolated, I feel that the team could play around and learn while still progressing towards the final project. My personal favourite was definitely Sprint 1 because it made us find the limits and edge cases of each endpoint.
The most difficult part was reflecting on each part of the process, including (but not limited to) code smells, design principles, etc. Due to this, I strongly believe I have gained skill in explaining my coding decisions/plans. The general context of teamwork also made for some interesting workload management lessons.
